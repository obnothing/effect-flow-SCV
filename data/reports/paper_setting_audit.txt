Scope:
This audit focuses on experiment settings that affect reproduction but are unclear or under-specified in the paper.

Key paper evidence:
- Shared encoder: the paper states that the sharing layer uses BERT for feature extraction and that opcodes are converted by Token, Segment, and Position Embeddings before the Transformer encoder.
- Dataset: BJUT SC01 contains 148,384 contracts and 10 target vulnerability labels. Table II reports the before/after MLSMOTE counts.
- Loss: the joint loss is the average of detection loss and recognition loss.
- MLSMOTE: the paper says MLSMOTE is applied only after splitting train/test, and only to the training set.
- Parameter settings: Table VIII reports epoch=80, batch size=64, learning rate=0.001, optimizer=Adam, dropout=0.5.
- Average metrics: in the shared-layer ablation, the paper says precision, recall, and F1 are calculated as averages over 10 vulnerabilities.

Most likely reasons our metrics are below the paper:

1. Split protocol mismatch is probably the largest factor. The paper does not specify a strict opcode-hash grouped split. Our split removes exact opcode overlap across train/valid/test, which is a harder and more leakage-resistant protocol.
2. The exact BERT model and tokenizer are not specified. Our CodeBERT-base BPE setup may not match their BERT tokenizer or possible custom opcode embedding/tokenizer.
3. MLSMOTE is not strictly reproduced. The paper describes synthetic kNN-based multi-label oversampling, but not the feature space. Our current training uses duplicate-based MLSMOTE-compatible oversampling.
4. Training hyperparameters differ strongly. Paper Table VIII uses epoch=80, batch size=64, LR=0.001, Adam, dropout=0.5. Our strongest baseline uses epochs=10, micro-batch=8, grad accumulation=2, LR=1e-5, AdamW, and weighted BCE.
5. Label mapping is not fully documented by the paper. The Unchecked call return value mapping remains a reproduction risk because the raw same-name column count does not match Table II.
6. Evaluation thresholding is unspecified. Multi-label F1 can change substantially with the threshold.

Settings that cannot be strictly reproduced from the paper alone:

- Concrete BERT checkpoint/model name.
- Tokenizer algorithm and vocabulary.
- Max sequence length and truncation policy.
- Whether BERT loads pretrained weights.
- Whether the shared BERT encoder is frozen or fine-tuned.
- Whether batch size 64 is micro-batch or effective batch.
- Whether gradient accumulation is used.
- Train/test/validation split ratio and random seed.
- Exact MLSMOTE feature representation and generation details.
- Raw 29-label to 10-label mapping rules.
- Multi-label decision threshold.
- Exact macro/micro averaging convention beyond "averages over 10 vulnerabilities".
- Full VTE fusion implementation beyond the equations.
